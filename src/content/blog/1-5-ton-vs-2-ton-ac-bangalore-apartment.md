**For a typical Bangalore apartment bedroom (120 – 180 sq ft), a 1.5 ton AC is the right size.** Go up to 2 ton only when the room is larger than 200 sq ft, is on the top floor under direct sun, has heavy west-facing exposure, or is a hall/living-dining combination. Bangalore's climate — moderate summers, cool nights, mild humidity — means the correct tonnage is usually one step *below* what a Delhi or Chennai sizing chart would suggest.

## The Bangalore-specific rule of thumb

Standard AC sizing guides across India use roughly **0.06 tons per square foot** — meaning a 150 sq ft room "needs" a 1 ton AC. That number is calibrated for Delhi/Chennai peak-summer conditions with 40°C+ outdoor temperatures.

Bangalore is different. Peak-summer highs rarely go above 35-37°C, and even those come with cool evenings. The practical Bangalore adjustment:

- **Up to 120 sq ft** → 1 ton is enough (bedroom, small study)
- **120 – 180 sq ft** → **1.5 ton is the sweet spot** (most master bedrooms)
- **180 – 220 sq ft** → 1.5 ton for a normal room, 2 ton for a top-floor or west-facing room
- **220+ sq ft** → 2 ton, or a 1.5 ton plus a ceiling fan (often the more efficient combo)

## When 2 ton is actually the right choice

Push up to 2 ton if any of these apply:

- **Room over 200 sq ft** — for example, a merged living-dining hall
- **Top-floor unit** with a directly-heated concrete slab above
- **Heavy west-facing exposure** with 3+ hours of afternoon sun
- **High occupancy** — more than 3-4 people regularly in the room
- **Kitchen adjacent** or open-plan kitchen — cooking heat load is real
- **Glass-heavy façade** — floor-to-ceiling windows or a full balcony wall

If none of those apply, a 2 ton AC in a 150 sq ft bedroom is oversized. Oversized ACs short-cycle (turn on and off frequently), which paradoxically cools less effectively, dehumidifies poorly, and wears the compressor faster.

## The oversizing trap

Buying "one size up for safety" is a common Bangalore apartment mistake. Here's what oversizing actually does:

- **Short-cycles the compressor** — reaches setpoint too fast, then shuts off before it's properly cooled the room's air mass
- **Poor dehumidification** — humidity control needs sustained runtime; short cycles leave the room clammy
- **Higher running cost** than a right-sized inverter, even though the star rating looks the same
- **Faster compressor wear** — more start/stop cycles = shorter unit lifespan
- **Higher upfront cost** for both purchase and installation

The one exception is if you're likely to expand the space in the next 2-3 years (e.g., breaking down a wall to combine bedroom and study) — then plan for the eventual room size, not today's.

## FAQs

**Is a 1.5 ton AC enough for a 12x14 ft Bangalore bedroom?** Yes — 168 sq ft falls squarely in 1.5 ton's sweet spot for Bangalore, regardless of floor.

**Should I go 2 ton for a top-floor bedroom in Bangalore?** Only if the room is larger than 180 sq ft AND you get significant afternoon sun. For a moderate top-floor bedroom under 180 sq ft, 1.5 ton with a ceiling fan performs better than a bare 2 ton.

**Does inverter vs fixed-speed change the tonnage decision?** No — tonnage is about the room; inverter vs fixed is about how efficiently the AC delivers that tonnage. Pick tonnage on room size, then pick 5-star inverter for lower bills over the AC's life.

**1.5 ton for a 200 sq ft hall — will it be enough?** Marginal. It will cool the room but will run continuously in peak summer, which is fine for a bedroom but often not enough for a hall where doors open frequently. 2 ton or a two-AC setup (1 ton + 1 ton) usually wins for halls above 200 sq ft.

**Split or window at each tonnage?** Both come in 1.5 and 2 ton. Split is quieter and more efficient; window is cheaper upfront and has no installation complications.
