"AC gas refill" is one of the most searched phrases when an air conditioner starts cooling poorly — but the honest answer surprises most people: a correctly installed, leak-free AC should almost never need a gas top-up.

## Refrigerant isn't consumed — it's sealed

Unlike fuel or engine oil, the refrigerant (gas) in your AC runs in a fully sealed loop between the indoor and outdoor units. It doesn't get used up through normal cooling. If your AC's refrigerant level has dropped, gas is escaping somewhere — through a leak in the copper piping, a joint, or the coil itself.

That means "needs gas refill" is really a symptom, and the real question is: **where is it leaking, and has that leak been fixed?**

## Signs your AC may be low on refrigerant

- Cooling feels weak even though the unit is running constantly
- Ice forms on the indoor unit or the copper pipes
- A hissing or bubbling sound near the indoor or outdoor unit
- Higher electricity bills for the same amount of cooling
- The compressor cycles more frequently than usual

## Why "just top it up" isn't a real fix

Simply adding more gas without finding and sealing the leak means you'll be back to weak cooling within months — and repeated top-ups cost more over a year than one proper leak repair. A correct service includes leak detection (often with a UV dye or electronic leak detector), sealing the leak, vacuum-testing the system, and then charging it to the manufacturer's specified refrigerant level.

## What type of gas does your AC use?

Most ACs installed in India over the last several years use **R32** or **R410A** refrigerant, both more efficient and less ozone-damaging than the older **R22**, which is being phased out. If you have an older R22 unit, ask your technician whether a retrofit or replacement makes more financial sense than continuing to service it.

## How often should a healthy AC need service?

A **general service** (cleaning filters, coils, and checking drainage) is recommended once or twice a year, especially before Bangalore's peak summer months. A **gas refill**, on the other hand, should be rare — if you're being told you need one every cooling season, ask specifically about leak detection before agreeing to another top-up.
